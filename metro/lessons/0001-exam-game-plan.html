Lesson 01 · 15 minutes

Exam game plan

Before any math: know exactly how the paper works, so you spend all three hours earning marks and none of it deciding what to do.

What the paper looks like

From your exam invitation:

  • Part I has four sections (Mathematics, Data Analytics, Logical Reasoning, Programming). Each has five questions, easiest first.
  • You complete two sections. If you answer more, only the first two answered are graded.
  • Part II is English: short paragraphs on a topic such as Smart Industry.
  • Exam time is 17:30–20:30 (3 hours). Pencil and eraser. A calculator can be borrowed from a supervisor.
Rule

Write Section 1 – Mathematics and Section 4 – Programming clearly at the top of your answers. Do not write anything in the other two sections. "First two answered" is ambiguous about order on paper, so remove all doubt.

The time budget

The invitation doesn't say whether Part II has its own time slot, so plan as if all of it fits inside the 3 hours. If staff say otherwise at the 17:00 briefing, you just gain time.

Inside each section, spend about 8 minutes on Q1–Q2 together and give the remaining time to Q3–Q5, which are harder and probably worth more. If a question stalls you for 5 minutes, move on and come back.

What gets you marks on paper

  • Show the working. A clear line of reasoning can earn partial credit even with a slip at the end. (This is standard practice for written exams; the invitation doesn't state the marking scheme.)
  • Box the final answer. Graders scan for it.
  • Practise by hand, without a calculator. The numbers in these questions are usually chosen to be friendly.
  • For pseudocode: readable beats clever. Indent, name variables well, state inputs and outputs. Lesson 07 covers this.

Check yourself

Q1

You finish Math and Programming with 20 minutes left. Logical Reasoning Q1 looks easy. What do you do?

Anything written there risks being counted as one of your "first two" sections. Use spare time to check your math signs and trace your pseudocode.
Q2

The window is 180 minutes. You give English 35 minutes and keep 10 for review. How many minutes does each Part I section get if you split the rest evenly?

(180 − 35 − 10) ÷ 2 = 135 ÷ 2 = 67.5. The plan above rounds to 65 each and adds 5 minutes for reading the paper first.
Q3

Which habit most improves your marks on a hard Q5 you can't fully finish?

Partial working shows the grader what you know. A bare number is either right or worth nothing.

Exam-day checklist

  • Passport or official ID (required)
  • Two pencils, eraser, sharpener. A watch is useful if phones must be away.
  • Address: Leiritie 1, 01600 Vantaa, building A, auditorium MMA101 (1st floor). Arrive by 16:45.
  • Water and a snack: it's a 3.5-hour evening.
  • Questions about the exam itself: the programme contact listed in the invitation.
