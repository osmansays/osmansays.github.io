Mean and median

Two ways to describe the "middle" of a dataset. The arithmetic is easy; the marks are lost on forgetting to sort, or on an even number of values.

Warm-up · from lesson 04

f(x) = x⁴ − 3x. Find f′(2).

f′(x) = 4x³ − 3, f′(2) = 32 − 3 = 29.

Definitions

  • Mean (average) = sum of values ÷ number of values.
  • Median = the middle value after sorting. With an even count, it's the mean of the two middle values. Position of the median: (n + 1) / 2.
  • Mode = the most frequent value (not listed in the spec, but a free mark if asked).
  • Range = max − min.

Background: OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e, section 2.5 "Measures of the Center of the Data".

Worked example

Data: 4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 8 (n = 6)

  1. Sort first: 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 8
  2. Mean = (3+4+5+6+8+8) / 6 = 34 / 6 ≈ 5.67
  3. n is even, so median = mean of the 3rd and 4th values = (5 + 6) / 2 = 5.5
  4. Mode = 8 (appears twice)

From a frequency table

A table "value · how many times" is just a compressed list. Mean = Σ(value × frequency) ÷ Σ frequency. For the median, count through the cumulative frequencies to the middle position.

Mean = (1·2 + 2·5 + 3·3) / 10 = 21 / 10 = 2.1. Ten values, so the median is between the 5th and 6th. Values 1–2 are "1", values 3–7 are "2" → median = 2.

The missing-value trick (likely harder variant)

If you know the mean and all but one value: total = mean × n, then subtract the known values.

Why have both?

The median ignores outliers; the mean doesn't. Readings 3, 4, 5, 6, 100 have mean 23.6 but median 5. For a sensor that occasionally spikes, the median describes "normal" better.

Practice

P1

Mean of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10?

30 / 5 = 6.
P2

Median of 7, 1, 9, 3, 5?

Sorted: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Middle (3rd) value = 5.
P3

Median of 12, 4, 9, 7?

Sorted: 4, 7, 9, 12. Even count → (7 + 9) / 2 = 8.
P4

Cycle times (s): 12, 15, 11, 18, 16, 14, 20, 13. Find the mean and median.

Sum = 119, n = 8 → mean = 14.875. Sorted: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 20 → median = (14 + 15) / 2 = 14.5.
P5

Five numbers have mean 10. Four of them are 8, 9, 11, 14. What's the fifth?

Total = 10 × 5 = 50. Known sum = 42. Missing = 8.
P6

Frequency table: value 1 appears 1 time, value 2 appears 4 times, value 3 appears 4 times, value 4 appears 1 time. Find mean and median.

Mean = (1 + 8 + 12 + 4) / 10 = 25 / 10 = 2.5. Ten values; 5th is 2 (positions 2–5), 6th is 3 (positions 6–9) → median = (2 + 3) / 2 = 2.5.
P7

A dataset gets one huge outlier added. What happens?

The outlier enters the sum, so the mean jumps. The median shifts by at most one position.
