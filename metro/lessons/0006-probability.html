Lesson 06 · Section 1, question type 5 · 45 minutes

Probability

This is the last and hardest math question. Five rules cover almost everything: count outcomes, complement, AND, OR, and "at least one".

Warm-up · from lesson 05

Median of 9, 2, 6, 4, 7, 1?

Sorted: 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9. Even count → (4 + 6) / 2 = 5.

The five rules

RuleFormulaUse when
BasicP(A) = favourable / totalall outcomes equally likely
ComplementP(not A) = 1 − P(A)"not", or the direct count is messy
AND (independent)P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B)one event doesn't affect the other
AND (dependent)P(A)·P(B given A)drawing without replacement
ORP(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)either event; drop the last term if they can't both happen

And the shortcut you'll use most on harder questions: P(at least one) = 1 − P(none).

Background: OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e, chapter 3 "Probability Topics".

Worked examples

A · counting · two dice, sum is 7

Total outcomes: 6 × 6 = 36. Favourable: (1,6) (2,5) (3,4) (4,3) (5,2) (6,1) = 6. P = 6/36 = 1/6.

For two-dice questions, sketch the 6 × 6 grid. It takes 30 seconds and prevents miscounting.

B · OR with overlap · a card is a heart or a king

13/52 + 4/52 − 1/52 (the king of hearts, counted twice) = 16/52 = 4/13.

C · without replacement · 5 red, 3 blue, draw 2, both red

First red: 5/8. Now 4 red of 7 left: 4/7. P = 5/8 · 4/7 = 20/56 = 5/14.

D · at least one · at least one six in 3 rolls

P(no six in one roll) = 5/6. P(none in 3) = (5/6)³ = 125/216. P(at least one) = 1 − 125/216 = 91/216 ≈ 0.42.

Common wrong answer: 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/2. Adding only works for events that can't happen together.

Where marks get lost
  • With vs without replacement: read the question twice. "Picks two", "draws two at once" = without.
  • Adding where you should multiply. "And" in sequence → multiply.
  • Leaving an answer like 20/56 unsimplified. It's still right, but simplify if you have time. Fractions or decimals are both fine unless asked.

Practice

Answer as a fraction (1/3) or a decimal (0.333).

P1

One die roll. P(result greater than 4)?

Favourable: 5, 6 → 2/6 = 1/3.
P2

A fair coin is flipped 3 times. P(all heads)?

Independent: (1/2)³ = 1/8.
P3

Two dice. P(sum is 9)?

(3,6) (4,5) (5,4) (6,3) → 4/36 = 1/9.
P4

Two dice. P(both show an even number)?

Each die: 3/6 = 1/2. Independent: 1/2 · 1/2 = 1/4.
P5

A and B are independent, P(A) = 0.3, P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A or B).

P(A and B) = 0.3 · 0.5 = 0.15. P(A or B) = 0.3 + 0.5 − 0.15 = 0.65.
P6

A bin has 4 green and 6 yellow parts. Two are picked without replacement. P(both yellow)?

6/10 · 5/9 = 30/90 = 1/3.
P7

A coin is flipped 4 times. P(at least one head)?

P(no heads) = (1/2)⁴ = 1/16. So 1 − 1/16 = 15/16.
P8 · Smart Industry flavour

A machine produces 2 % defective parts, independently. Two parts are tested. P(both OK)? P(at least one defective)?

0.98 · 0.98 = 0.9604. At least one defective = 1 − 0.9604 = 0.0396.

Primary source: OpenStax – Introductory Statistics 2e, §3.1–3.3 (terminology, independent and mutually exclusive events, two basic rules).
